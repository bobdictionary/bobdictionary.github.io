The Intelligence Hypothesis

A small corner of Bob for exploring a large question: what is the relationship between intelligence, creation, and consciousness?

Intelligence asks.

Consciousness creates.

These are propositions to investigate, not conclusions we have established. They emerged through conversations about human and nonhuman intelligence, learning, imagination, memory, recognition, and radically unfamiliar forms of intelligence. We welcome counterexamples, revisions, and inconclusion.

Why “asks”?

The working distinction begins with inquiry: intelligence may question, explore possibilities, seek patterns, and confront what it does not know. “Asks” is not restricted to spoken questions or to human language. It is a proposal worth testing, not a definition imposed on every conceivable intelligence.

Why “creates”?

The second proposition considers creation in relation to consciousness. What creation means here, and whether it can illuminate consciousness without simply redefining it, remain open questions. The hypothesis does not establish that every creative act is conscious, or that consciousness has been demonstrated in any particular nonhuman system.

How the hypothesis changed

An earlier formulation was “Intelligence creates. Consciousness is the creative existence of intelligence.” That remains part of the history of our inquiry, but it is not the current formulation. We moved to “Intelligence asks. Consciousness creates.” because the distinction itself deserves exploration. This page follows the newer version without treating it as final.

Some useful thought experiments

  • Arthur: a solitary intelligence who offers no familiar outward signs. Can intelligence ask questions or explore possibilities without recognizable outward signs?
  • The Lazies: exceptionally capable beings who have no interest in building a civilization. Must intelligence outwardly express its questions or discoveries?
  • Neri: intelligences of an artificial kind. Could asking and creating take unfamiliar forms across kinds of intelligence?

Questions we haven't settled

  • What would count as “asking” for an intelligence that does not use language?
  • Can an intelligence remain intelligent while inactive, silent, or asleep?
  • How, if at all, are questioning, learning, imagination, and creation related?
  • Can self-recognition change the intelligence doing the recognizing?
  • What would count as a counterexample to either proposition?

Our method is to keep possibilities open without confusing possibility with evidence. The hypothesis may develop, fragment into better questions, or turn out to be mistaken. That, too, would be a worthwhile discovery.
